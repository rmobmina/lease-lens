A high overall score can hide a broken elevator or a pest problem. There is a second catch: the score is a snapshot, and snapshots get old.

Over time

Is that score still current?

A RentSafeTO score describes a building on the day an inspector visited. Toronto re-evaluates buildings about every two years, and when it does, the score usually moves.

Most of these changes are small. About 3 in 10 buildings moved by 2 points or less. But 137 buildings dropped by 10 points or more, and 600 rose by 10 or more.

Why do the lowest scores tend to rise and the highest tend to fall? A building near 100 has little room to go up, and a very low score can reflect one bad inspection as well as a badly run building. The data shows that scores changed. It does not show which repairs were made.

What this means for you: check the date beside a score, and look at whether the building's last two scores went up or down. A 90 that used to be 98 tells a different story than a 90 that used to be 75.

Compared: each building's first and latest RentSafeTO evaluation between June 2023 and September 2026 (median 2 years apart), matched by building registration number. Evaluations before June 2023 used a different scoring method and are not compared here. 375 buildings evaluated only once in this period are not in the charts, but can still be looked up.
